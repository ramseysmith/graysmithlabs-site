What holiday is it on December 18?

December 18 is National Ugly Sweater Day, National Roast Suckling Pig Day and World Arabic Language Day. It falls on a Friday in 2026 and a Saturday in 2027.

Written and illustrated by Graysmith Labs, the makers of CelebriDay.

Fun

National Ugly Sweater Day

A fun holiday tradition where people proudly don their most outrageous and festive holiday sweaters.

National Ugly Sweater Day falls on the third Friday of December and celebrates the gloriously tacky holiday sweater. Bulky Christmas knits with reindeer and snowflakes were popular in the 1980s, faded, then came back as a joke in the early 2000s when friends began throwing ugly sweater parties.

Now the sweaters are loud on purpose, with tinsel, bells and blinking lights. Many schools and offices use the day to raise money, like Save the Children's Christmas Jumper Day in the UK.

Ways to celebrate

  • Wear your loudest holiday sweater all day.
  • Host an ugly sweater contest with silly prizes.
  • Decorate a plain sweater with felt, pom poms and bells.

The date moves each year. In 2026 it falls on Friday, December 18. After that: December 17 in 2027, December 15 in 2028, December 21 in 2029 and December 20 in 2030.

Food

National Roast Suckling Pig Day

A day celebrating the ancient culinary tradition of roasting a whole young pig to golden, crackling perfection.

Nobody is sure who started National Roast Suckling Pig Day, but whole roast pig is a feast dish in many cultures. Spain's Segovia is famous for cochinillo, the Philippines for lechón, and Puerto Rico and Cuba for lechón asado at Christmas and big family gatherings.

A suckling pig is a very young pig, which is why the meat is so tender and the skin so thin. Slow roasting renders the fat under the skin and turns it into glassy crackling. In Segovia, some cooks famously cut the cooked pig with the edge of a plate to show how tender it is.

Ways to celebrate

  • Roast pork shoulder with crackling skin.
  • Try lechón or cochinillo at a local restaurant.
  • Make a citrus garlic mojo marinade.

Culture

World Arabic Language Day

A UNESCO day celebrating the Arabic language, one of the world's oldest and most widely spoken languages.

December 18 is the date in 1973 when the United Nations General Assembly made Arabic one of its official languages. UNESCO has led the celebration each year since 2012.

Arabic is written from right to left in a script of 28 letters, most of which change shape depending on where they sit in a word. Arabic calligraphy is a celebrated art form. Many English words came through Arabic, including algebra, coffee, sugar and alcohol, a sign of how much knowledge passed through the Arabic speaking world.

Ways to celebrate

  • Learn to write your name in Arabic script.
  • Listen to music sung in Arabic.
  • Cook a dish from an Arabic speaking country.
