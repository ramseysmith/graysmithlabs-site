Food

Thanksgiving

A day to gather with family and friends, share a big meal, and give thanks for the good things in life.

In the fall of 1621, the Pilgrims of Plymouth Colony and about 90 Wampanoag men led by Massasoit shared a harvest feast that lasted three days. It was not called Thanksgiving at the time, and the menu likely had venison and wild fowl rather than the dinner we know now.

Days of thanks came and went for two centuries, until the writer Sarah Josepha Hale campaigned for a national holiday. President Abraham Lincoln proclaimed one in 1863, and a 1941 law fixed it on the fourth Thursday of November. For many Native Americans it is also a day of remembrance.

Ways to celebrate

  • Go around the table and share one thing you are thankful for.
  • Invite someone who would otherwise spend the day alone.
  • Take a walk together between dinner and dessert.

The date moves each year. In 2026 it falls on Thursday, November 26. After that: November 25 in 2027, November 23 in 2028, November 22 in 2029 and November 28 in 2030.

Food

National Cake Day

A sweet and celebratory day devoted to all kinds of cakes in every flavor.

Nobody is sure who started National Cake Day, but few foods are tied to as many happy moments. Cakes mark birthdays, weddings, graduations and holidays in cultures all over the world, from layered sponges to dense pound cakes to cheesecakes.

Many cakes we know today owe their light texture to chemical leaveners. Before baking powder and baking soda were common in the 1800s, bakers relied on yeast or on beating eggs for a long time to trap air. That is one reason older cake recipes can read like a workout, with instructions to beat the batter for an hour.

Ways to celebrate

  • Bake a simple cake from scratch.
  • Bring a slice to a coworker or neighbor.
  • Try a cake style from another country.

About national days

Are national days official?

Most are not. In the United States only Congress and the president create federal holidays, and there are eleven of them. The rest are proclaimed by presidents, governors or mayors, adopted by the United Nations, or started by a club, a company or one stubborn person who kept at it.

What is the difference between a holiday and an observance?

A holiday usually means time off: federal offices and many businesses close. An observance is a date people mark without closing anything, such as World Oceans Day or National Pickle Day.

Who decides when a national day falls?

Whoever starts it. Some pick a birthday, as World Diabetes Day did with Frederick Banting's. Some pick a joke: Mad Hatter Day is October 6 because the Hatter's price tag reads 10/6. Others float, such as Thanksgiving on the fourth Thursday in November.

How do you check these dates?

We publish a day only after finding its date at a source we can name, and each page links to it. Days that failed the check are left off, even when other lists carry them. If you spot a mistake, write to us and we will fix it.
