What holiday is it on January 1?

January 1 is New Year's Day and First Foot Day. It falls on a Thursday in 2026 and a Friday in 2027.

Culture

New Year's Day

The first day of the calendar year, celebrated around the world with fireworks, gatherings, and fresh starts.

January 1 became the start of the year in Rome when Julius Caesar's new Julian calendar took effect in 45 BC. The month is named for Janus, the Roman god of doorways and beginnings, often shown with two faces, one looking back and one looking ahead. Not everyone kept the date, though. England and its American colonies officially began the year on March 25 until the 1750s.

Many cultures still celebrate a new year on other dates, such as Lunar New Year, Nowruz at the spring equinox and Rosh Hashanah in autumn. Because of time zones, the Line Islands of Kiribati are among the very first places on Earth to reach January 1 each year.

Ways to celebrate

  • Write down one goal and one small first step.
  • Take a fresh air walk on the first morning.
  • Call someone you missed last year.

Culture

First Foot Day

A Scottish tradition where the first person to enter a home after midnight on New Year's brings gifts for good luck.

First footing is part of Hogmanay, the Scottish celebration of the New Year. Nobody is sure exactly when it began, but the belief is old: the first person to cross your threshold after midnight sets your household's luck for the year ahead. Friends and neighbors would go from house to house through the night, first footing one another.

The first foot traditionally brings small symbolic gifts. A lump of coal promised warmth, salt or bread promised enough food, and a dram of whisky promised good cheer. Some visitors brought shortbread or black bun, a rich fruit cake baked inside pastry. In return the visitor was given food and a drink before moving on to the next house.

Ways to celebrate

  • Bring a small gift to the first home you visit.
  • Bake shortbread to share with neighbors.
  • Learn all the words to Auld Lang Syne.
