What holiday is it on February 29?

Leap Day

The bonus day that shows up once every four years to keep our calendar in step with the Earth's orbit around the Sun.

A year on Earth is about 365 and a quarter days long, so every four years the calendar adds February 29 to stay in step with the seasons. Julius Caesar's calendar added a leap day from 45 BCE, and the Gregorian calendar of 1582 refined the rule: century years skip it unless they divide by 400, which is why 2000 had one and 1900 did not.

In old Irish and British tradition, Leap Day was the one day a woman could propose to a man. People born today, called leaplings, often celebrate on February 28 or March 1 in other years.

Ways to celebrate

  • Do something you have never done, since today is a bonus day.
  • Write a letter to yourself to open on the next Leap Day.
  • Wish a happy birthday to anyone born on February 29.

The date moves each year. In 2028 it falls on Tuesday, February 29.
