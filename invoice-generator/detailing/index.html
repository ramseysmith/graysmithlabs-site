How to bill auto detailing

  1. Lead with the package. Clients book a package (interior, exterior, full). Put it first, with the vehicle size in the line, since size sets the price.
  2. Add ons each get a line. Pet hair, stains, headlights, engine bay and coatings change the job. Listing them separately shows why this detail cost more than the last.
  3. Write the vehicle on the invoice. Make, model and color in the client details make the invoice easy to match later, especially for clients with more than one car.
  4. Note a mobile or travel fee. If you come to the client, a travel fee on its own line covers the drive and water.

Questions

What should a car detailing invoice include?

Your business name and contact details, the client, the vehicle (make, model, color), the date, the package with its price, each add on, any travel fee, the total and how to pay.

How do detailers price by vehicle size?

Most use three tiers: car, midsize SUV and truck or large SUV, with the same package costing more for each. Put the size in the package line so the price makes sense.

Should a detailer take payment before or after?

After the client has seen the car, with the invoice handed over or texted on the spot. For ceramic coatings and long jobs, many take a deposit when booking.
