How to bill lawn care

  1. Price the visit, not the minutes. Most clients expect a flat price per mow for a lot size they know. Keep hourly rates for cleanups and jobs that change each time.
  2. Put extras on their own lines. Edging, leaf cleanup, mulch and haul away each get a line with a quantity, so the client sees what changed from last week.
  3. Bill weekly or monthly, the same way every time. Regular clients often prefer one invoice at the end of the month listing every visit by date. One off jobs get an invoice the same day.
  4. Write down what you used. Bags of mulch, fertilizer and seed go on as materials at your price, which keeps a busy season from eating your margin.

Questions

What should a lawn care invoice include?

Your business name and contact details, the client and the property address, the visit date, each service on its own line (mowing, edging, cleanup, materials) with a quantity and price, the total, the due date and how to pay.

Should I charge for lawn care per hour or per visit?

Per visit for regular mowing, because clients like a price they can plan on and you earn more as you get faster. Per hour for cleanups, hedge work and anything you cannot see before you start.

How do I bill clients I mow for every week?

Either send a short invoice after each visit, or one monthly invoice with a line per visit date. Pick one, tell the client up front and keep the same day each month so they know when it is coming.
