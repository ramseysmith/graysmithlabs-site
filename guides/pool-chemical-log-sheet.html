What is on the log sheet

Page one has 20 rows, enough for a month of tests at five a week, or a busy fortnight at a pool tested every day. The range to aim for sits under each heading.

Ranges differ a little between test kit makers, and the label on your chemicals comes first when it disagrees with a range here. Salt pools make chlorine, so use the chlorine range.

Public, HOA and rental pools

Pools open to the public, to an HOA, or to guests usually have to keep a daily chemical log because the local health code asks for one. The code also sets the minimum chlorine, the pH range, how often to test and how long to keep the records, and some inspectors want their own form.

Check with your county or city health department before you rely on this sheet for a public pool. Where it accepts any log with the required readings, the Initials column and the Combined chlorine column cover what inspectors most often look for.

The maintenance checklist on page two

  • Every day the pool is open: test free chlorine and pH, skim, empty the skimmer baskets, check the pump and water level.
  • Two to three times a week: brush walls, steps and floor, and read the filter pressure gauge.
  • Every week: test alkalinity, vacuum, and shock when combined chlorine reaches 0.5 ppm.
  • Every month: test calcium hardness and stabilizer, and clean or backwash the filter once pressure is 8 to 10 psi over its clean reading.

How to read your own log

  • Free chlorine is gone by the next morning: sunlight is burning it off (check stabilizer) or the pool needs a shock.
  • pH keeps climbing: common with salt pools and new plaster. Lower it in small doses and retest.
  • Combined chlorine rises while free chlorine looks fine: the water needs a shock, not more daily chlorine.
  • Stabilizer only goes up: it does not burn off, so the fix is to drain part of the water and refill.

Write down what you added as well as the reading. The log is only useful when you can see what each dose did.

Questions

Is the pool chemical log sheet free to copy?

Yes. Print as many as you like for your home pool, HOA or front desk. No email or account is needed.

What should I record in a pool chemical log?

The date and time, free chlorine, combined chlorine, pH, alkalinity, calcium and stabilizer when you test them, the water temperature and clarity, what you added and how much, and the initials of the person who tested.

How often should I test pool water?

Free chlorine and pH every day the pool is in use, or at least two to three times a week for a quiet home pool. Alkalinity weekly, calcium and stabilizer monthly. Public pools follow the testing schedule in the local health code.

Does a public or HOA pool need a chemical log?

Usually yes. Most local health codes ask public and semi public pools to log chlorine and pH several times a day and keep the records for inspection. Ask your health department which readings and form it wants.

Is there an app for a pool log?

Pool test kit makers have free apps tied to their own test strips. Our app, TubTender, keeps the log for hot tubs and swim spas, not pools, so for a pool this printable sheet is the free option we offer.
