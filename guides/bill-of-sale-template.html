Bill of sale template (free printable PDF)

Download the bill of sale template (PDF): one page for a private sale of a car, trailer, boat or other item, with the seller, buyer, item, price, an as is line, an odometer line, signatures and an optional notary block. Before you use it, check your state DMV, because many states publish their own bill of sale form.

Each person keeps a signed copy. Take photos of both IDs and the signed page if you sell to a stranger.

Check your state first

A bill of sale is a receipt for a private sale. For a titled vehicle, the title transfer is what changes ownership, and the bill of sale sits beside it. States differ on the details: some publish their own bill of sale form and expect it, some put a bill of sale section on the title itself, some ask for a notary, and some ask for one only for certain sales.

Search for your state name plus "DMV bill of sale" and use the state form when there is one. This template fits the cases where the state accepts any written bill of sale, and sales of things with no title at all, such as furniture, a bike, a camera or a small trailer in many states.

This page and the template are general information, not legal advice. For a high value sale or anything unusual, ask the DMV or a lawyer in your state.

How to fill it in

  1. Print the PDF, or open it on your phone and type into it (see the next section).
  2. Copy the VIN, hull ID or serial number from the item itself, not from memory, and check it matches the title.
  3. Write the real price. A made up low price can cause trouble with sales tax and is not worth it.
  4. Fill in the as is line, or write any promise the seller makes, such as a new battery.
  5. Both people sign and date it at the time of the sale, and each keeps a copy.

Signing a bill of sale on your phone

You do not need a printer. Save the PDF to the Files app, tap Markup, add text boxes on the lines and add your signature. It works, but placing every text box by hand is slow.

SignSnap for iPhone signs a PDF in a few taps with no account: import the bill of sale from Files, or scan a filled in paper copy with the camera, drag your saved signature onto the line, stamp the date beside it and share the signed PDF by Mail, Messages or AirDrop. The other person then signs their line on their own phone the same way, or you both sign one printed copy.

Selling a car: what else to do

  • Sign over the title in the seller section, exactly as the title asks, with the odometer reading where it has a space for it.
  • Remove your plates if your state keeps plates with the seller.
  • Tell the DMV you sold it, if your state has a notice of sale or release of liability form. This keeps later tickets and tolls off your record.
  • Cancel or move your insurance after the buyer drives away.

Questions

Is this bill of sale template free?

Yes. Print or copy it as often as you like. No email or account is needed.

Does my state have its own bill of sale form?

Many states do, usually on the DMV or motor vehicle website, and some put a bill of sale section on the title. Search for your state name plus "DMV bill of sale" and use that form when there is one.

Does a bill of sale need to be notarized?

In most states, no, but a few states ask for a notarized bill of sale for some vehicle or boat sales. Your state DMV says whether yours does. The template has an optional notary block for those cases.

Is a handwritten bill of sale legal?

In many states a handwritten bill of sale is accepted when it has the same details: names, the item and its VIN or serial number, the price, the date and both signatures. Check your state DMV, since some states ask for their own form.

Can I sign a bill of sale on my phone?

Yes, where an electronic signature is accepted. Open the PDF in SignSnap or in the Files app Markup tool, add your signature and date, and send it to the other person to sign. Some DMV offices still want ink signatures on the title itself.

What does sold as is mean?

The buyer takes the item in its current condition and the seller makes no promise about it, unless something is written on the bill of sale.
