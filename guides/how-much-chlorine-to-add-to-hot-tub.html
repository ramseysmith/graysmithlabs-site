How much chlorine to add to a hot tub

For a typical 400 gallon hot tub, about 1¾ teaspoons (0.3 oz) of 56 percent dichlor granules, or about 2½ fl oz of 6 percent unscented bleach, raises free chlorine by 3 ppm, enough to take a reading of 1 ppm up to the 4 ppm target. As a rule of thumb, half a teaspoon of dichlor per 100 gallons adds about 3.5 ppm. Keep free chlorine between 3 and 10 ppm, the range CDC guidance gives for hot tubs, and retest 20 minutes after each dose.

Updated November 25, 2026 by Graysmith Labs

A typical label shock dose is about 1 teaspoon of dichlor per 100 gallons, which adds roughly 7 ppm. Use it after a busy soak, then wait until a retest shows 10 ppm or less before anyone gets in.

Step by step

  1. Test first with a strip or drop kit and write down free chlorine and pH. If pH is above 7.8, lower it first: chlorine is much weaker at high pH.
  2. Know your tub's volume in gallons. It is in the manual or on the maker's site; most two to six seat tubs hold 250 to 600 gallons.
  3. Read the strength on your product's label: the percent of available chlorine for granules, or the percent of sodium hypochlorite for bleach.
  4. Work out the dose with the free hot tub calculator, or use the table above. Start with a little less than you think; you can always add more.
  5. Add it with the cover open and the jets running. Sprinkle granules slowly across the water, or pour bleach in front of a jet.
  6. Wait 20 minutes with the jets on, then retest. Soak only when free chlorine reads 3 to 10 ppm and pH 7.2 to 7.8.

How often to add chlorine

  • Before each soak: test, and top up if free chlorine is under 3 ppm.
  • After each soak: bodies, lotion and sweat use chlorine up fast in hot water, so a small dose after use keeps the next reading in range.
  • At least two or three times a week when the tub sits unused, because heat and the jets slowly burn chlorine off.
  • After a heavy use day or a party: a full shock dose, then wait for it to drop to 10 ppm or less before soaking.

Dichlor or bleach?

Both work. Dichlor granules (sodium dichloroisocyanurate, sold as spa shock or chlorinating granules) store well and barely move pH, but each dose also adds stabilizer (cyanuric acid), about 0.9 ppm for every 1 ppm of chlorine. Stabilizer builds up over weeks and makes chlorine slower; once it passes about 50 ppm, drain part of the water and refill.

Plain unscented bleach adds no stabilizer, costs little and is easy to measure, but it nudges pH up a little and loses strength as it ages, so buy it fresh and use the regular kind, not splashless or scented. Many owners use dichlor for daily doses and switch to bleach for a while when stabilizer gets high.

Skip chlorine pucks made for pools (trichlor): they are very acidic and build stabilizer quickly. Calcium hypochlorite raises calcium and can cloud spa water.

CDC recommends not using chlorine products that contain cyanuric acid, dichlor included, in hot tubs, because the stabilizer slows disinfection (CDC home hot tub page). Many home owners and spa brands still use dichlor. To follow CDC, use plain unscented bleach.

Common mistakes

  • Reading total chlorine instead of free chlorine. Free chlorine is the part that sanitizes; dose from that number.
  • Adding chlorine to water with pH above 7.8. At pH 8 only about a quarter of free chlorine is in its strong form, against about half at 7.5.
  • Closing the cover right after a dose. Leave it open with the jets on for 20 minutes so gases escape.
  • Getting in above 10 ppm. High chlorine irritates skin, eyes and lungs; wait for it to come down.
  • Guessing the dose from a pool chart. Hot tubs are small, so pool amounts overshoot badly.

Questions

How much chlorine do I put in my hot tub?

Enough to keep free chlorine between 3 and 10 ppm. For a 400 gallon tub, about 1¾ teaspoons of 56 percent dichlor granules or about 2½ fl oz of 6 percent bleach raises it by 3 ppm.

How much chlorine per 100 gallons in a hot tub?

Half a teaspoon of 56 percent dichlor granules per 100 gallons raises free chlorine by about 3.5 ppm. A full teaspoon per 100 gallons, the usual shock dose, adds about 7 ppm.

What should the chlorine level be in a hot tub?

Free chlorine 3 to 10 ppm, with pH 7.2 to 7.8. Those are the ranges in CDC hot tub guidance.

Can I use regular bleach in my hot tub?

Yes, plain unscented bleach works. Check its strength on the label (often 6 or 8.25 percent), dose by that, and avoid scented or splashless bleach.

How long after adding chlorine can I get in the hot tub?

Usually about 20 minutes with the jets running, once a retest shows free chlorine at 10 ppm or less and pH between 7.2 and 7.8.

Why does my hot tub chlorine keep dropping?

Heat, jets and bathers use it up quickly. High pH, too much stabilizer or old water also make it fade faster. Test before each soak and dose after use.
