Hot tub pH too high? How to lower it

When hot tub pH reads above 7.8, check total alkalinity first: if it is above 120 ppm, lower alkalinity instead, or pH will climb straight back. If alkalinity is in range, add pH decreaser (dry acid) with the pump running and the air jets off. For a 400 gallon tub at pH 8.0 with alkalinity at 90 ppm, about 2¾ teaspoons (0.5 oz) brings it to about 7.5; add half, wait 20 minutes, retest, then add the rest if needed.

Higher alkalinity needs more acid for the same change. Strip readings are coarse, so split every dose in half and retest between halves.

Step by step

  1. Test total alkalinity and pH. Alkalinity above 120 ppm? Follow how to lower hot tub alkalinity instead; it brings pH down along the way.
  2. Work out the dose with the free hot tub calculator or the table above, using the strength on your label.
  3. Turn the circulation pump on and the air jets and blower off. Air raises pH, so it would undo part of the dose.
  4. Add half the dose. Pour dry acid slowly over the water or dissolve it in a bucket of tub water first. Add muriatic acid to water, never water to acid, with gloves and eye protection.
  5. Wait 20 minutes and retest. Still above 7.8? Add the second half and retest again.
  6. Once pH reads 7.2 to 7.8, check sanitizer and top it up if it is low. High pH may have let it fade.

Why pH keeps going up

  • Jets and the air blower. Aeration lets carbon dioxide escape, and pH rises a little every time the tub runs. A slow upward drift is normal.
  • High total alkalinity. It pushes pH up and resists every dose. Fix it first.
  • Bleach and pH increaser. Liquid chlorine nudges pH up, and an extra scoop of soda ash after a misread strip is a common cause.
  • Fresh fill water. Many areas have tap water at pH 8 or higher, so a new fill often needs a first dose.

What high pH does

Target pH is 7.2 to 7.8, the range in CDC hot tub guidance. Above it:

  • Chlorine and bromine get much weaker. At pH 8 only about a quarter of free chlorine is in its strong form, against about half at 7.5.
  • Calcium comes out of the water as scale on the shell, jets and heater.
  • The water turns cloudy and can leave skin itchy or dry. See cloudy hot tub water if it looks hazy.

Common mistakes

  • Dumping in the whole label dose at once. Big doses overshoot pH below 7.2, which stings eyes and wears on the heater.
  • Running the air jets while the acid goes in. That raises pH again before the acid has done its work.
  • Using vinegar or lemon juice. They barely move pH in a hot tub and add organics the sanitizer has to burn off.
  • Retesting right away. Give it 20 minutes of circulation or the reading will not be settled.

Questions

How do I lower the pH in my hot tub?

Add pH decreaser (dry acid) or muriatic acid with the pump running and the air jets off. Add half the dose, wait 20 minutes, retest, then add the rest if pH is still above 7.8.

What happens if hot tub pH is too high?

Chlorine and bromine work much less well, scale forms on the shell and heater, and the water can turn cloudy and dry out skin.

Is it safe to use a hot tub with high pH?

It is better to wait. Above 7.8 the sanitizer is weak, so germs are not killed as fast. Lower pH to 7.2 to 7.8 first.

Why does my hot tub pH keep rising?

Aeration from the jets raises pH a little every day. High alkalinity, bleach and high pH tap water make it rise faster.

Should I lower alkalinity or pH first?

Alkalinity first when it is above 120 ppm. Lowering it brings pH down too, and pH set before alkalinity drifts back within a day.

Can I use vinegar to lower hot tub pH?

No. It barely moves pH in a hot tub and adds organics the sanitizer has to burn off. Use a spa pH decreaser.
