The findings

1. The paywall did not fit on one screen

High · Subscriptions and paywall

What we found. At 393 by 852 points, a common iPhone size, the feature list ran under the plans. The thing a person came to decide on was competing with a scroll.

What to do. Rebuild on one screen: art, benefits, both plans, the trial line and the button visible together at 375 by 667, 393 by 852 and 440 by 956 points.

Status. Fixed November 20.

2. Updates asked the ad network for test ads

High · Ads and privacy

What we found. Over the air updates ran with the app environment set to development, so they requested Google test ad units. The store build was configured correctly, which is why nothing looked wrong.

What to do. Set the production environment for every update, and stop any update that is not set from publishing.

Status. Fixed November 20, with a check in the publish script.

3. Every launch could wait behind the splash

High · Code and builds

What we found. The splash held for up to 12 seconds while looking for an update, on every launch. A slow network made the app feel broken.

What to do. Check once per install, for 3 seconds at most, then open.

Status. Fixed in 1.9.2.

4. Four ways into the paywall shared one generic headline

Medium · Subscriptions and paywall

What we found. Home (two places), Settings and the success screen all opened on the same line, the product name plus Premium. Neither was told why the screen had opened.

What to do. Give each entry point its own headline, with and without a trial.

Status. Fixed November 20.

5. The rating prompt came too late

Medium · Listing and search

What we found. The app asked for a rating only after many sessions. Most people sign one document and leave.

What to do. Ask after the first signed document is exported, the moment the app has done its job.

Status. Fixed November 12.

6. Nobody could tell why a paywall failed

Medium · Subscriptions and paywall

What we found. Views were counted, but not whether the plans had loaded, whether a trial was offered, or whether a purchase was started and cancelled.

What to do. Record plans loaded, trial shown, purchase tapped, cancelled and failed, each tied to the entry point.

Status. Fixed November 13.

7. Search words are out of reach, and 8 characters are unused

Medium · Listing and search

What we found. The keyword field uses 92 of 100 characters, and none of our 14 tracked words ranks in the top 25. The words we chose belong to far larger apps.

What to do. Add maker, so the listing can match signature maker, then test narrower words one listing at a time.

Status. Open, planned for the next version.

Questions

What does the $499 app audit cover?

Five areas: App Review risk, subscriptions and the paywall, ads and privacy, the store listing and search, and the code and builds. You get a written report ranked by what to fix first, then a 30 minute walkthrough call.

What do you need from me?

The app link to start. For a code review, read access to the repository; for store and revenue findings, view only access to App Store Connect or RevenueCat, or screenshots of them. The audit can run on the public app and listing alone, with less depth.

How long does it take?

The written report arrives within 5 business days of access. The walkthrough call is booked after you have read it.

Do you fix what you find?

The audit is the report and the call. You can fix everything yourself from it. If you want us to do the work, it is quoted separately as a fixed price.

Will an audit raise my revenue?

It tells you what is broken and what to try first. Nobody can promise a revenue number from a review, and we do not.
