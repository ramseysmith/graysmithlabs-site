Reaction time test

Tap or click the box the moment it turns green. After five rounds you get your average and best reaction time in milliseconds; most adults average about 250 to 300 ms on a test like this, and under 200 ms is very fast.

    What is a good reaction time?

    Use this as a rough guide for a tap or click test in a browser. Screens, touch sensors and browsers each add a little delay, so the fairest comparison is with your own earlier scores on the same device.

    AverageWhat it means
    Under 200 msVery fast. Typical of practiced gamers on a quick screen.
    200 to 250 msQuick. Faster than most people.
    250 to 300 msAbout average for adults on a test like this.
    300 to 350 msA little slow. Tiredness, distraction or a slow screen often explain it.
    Over 350 msSlow for this test. Try again rested, on a different device, with one finger ready.

    How to get a faster score

    • Hover, do not rest. Keep one finger or the mouse just above the box so the tap is a short drop.
    • Watch the whole box, not one spot. The color change is easier to catch in your wider vision.
    • Do not guess. The wait before green is random, so jumping early only restarts the round.
    • Test rested. Sleep and focus change scores more than most tips: a tired try is often 30 to 50 ms slower.
    • Practice on a target. Spotting the right thing and tapping it fast is a skill you can train. That is the whole idea of BlitzTap.

    This test is for fun and practice, not a medical or driving fitness test.

    Questions

    What is a good reaction time?

    In a tap or click test like this one, most adults average about 250 to 300 milliseconds. Under 250 ms is quick, and under 200 ms is very fast. Your device adds some delay too, so compare your own scores on the same phone or computer.

    What is the average human reaction time?

    For a simple visual signal, such as a screen turning green, typical adult averages fall around 200 to 300 milliseconds. Online tests usually land near the top of that range because the screen, the touch sensor and the browser each add a little delay.

    How do I test my reaction time?

    Tap or click the box above when it turns from red to green. The test runs five rounds with a random wait before each one, then shows your average and your best time in milliseconds. Tapping before green counts as too early and that round restarts.

    Can you improve your reaction time?

    Practice on a task makes you faster at that task, and sleep, rest and focus matter more than most people expect: a tired or distracted test is often 30 to 50 ms slower. Games that ask you to spot and tap a target quickly, like BlitzTap, are a fun way to practice.

    Why is my reaction time slower on a phone?

    Touch screens sample your finger a set number of times per second and the browser then has to draw the next frame, so a phone often adds a few tens of milliseconds compared with a fast mouse and monitor. Close other apps, use one finger and keep it hovering just above the box.

    Does reaction time get slower with age?

    Yes, simple reaction time tends to slow gradually through adulthood, and large studies find that sleep, health and practice also make a real difference. Compare yourself with your own past scores rather than with someone else's.
